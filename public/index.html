Methodology

Data, rates, and benchmark Q&A
How is the US cash rate used?

US funds use SOFR where available, blended with Fed Funds before SOFR history starts. The daily financing contribution is (leverage - 1) x annual rate x calendar days / 365.

How is the Taiwan cash rate used?

Taiwan funds use the TWD interbank overnight rate with the same financing formula.

What is the daily leverage benchmark basis?

The benchmark daily return is calculated from the official target-index close. The daily leveraged target is leverage x that benchmark daily return.

What price is used for the ETF?

US ETFs use adjusted close so distributions and splits are reinvested. 00631L uses TWSE official daily close with the 2026 split adjusted, because Yahoo's adjusted history is not reliable for that split.

What is theoretical return before fund costs?

It compounds the daily leveraged target after the estimated cash-rate contribution, but before expense ratio, swap or futures basis, trading costs, tracking error, and taxes.

ETF adjusted close and target-index close

The ETF side is pulled from adjusted close, so ETF distributions and splits are treated as reinvested into the same fund instead of being mis-classified as tracking drag.

The benchmark side uses the official target index close: Nasdaq-100 Index and S&P 500 Index. This is closer to the daily target definition than using QQQ or SPY as benchmark proxies.

This is still a pre-tax convention. It does not model dividend withholding tax, income tax, transaction costs from reinvesting ETF dividends, or the timing/cash drag an individual investor may face.

Why the model difference fluctuates

Daily model difference is leverage × target-index close return − ETF adjusted-close return. The rolling chart annualizes the sum of those daily gaps inside a 365-calendar-day window.

The line can jump when an extreme market day enters or leaves the rolling window.

These swings are plausible for leveraged funds because execution timing, NAV calculation, swap/futures basis, market disruption, rebalance cost, and benchmark pricing differences can all be large on volatile days.

Annualized model differences

Summary tracking cards use the selected date range. Model - ETF difference annualizes the compounded gap between theoretical and actual returns; financing impact is still summed by calendar day.

The tracking charts still show rolling 365-calendar-day annualized lines so changes over time remain visible.

Model - ETF difference

Difference between the theoretical return before fund costs and the actual ETF return. Positive means the model return was higher than the ETF; negative means the ETF beat the model.

Volatility decay / compounding effect

Daily-resetting leverage compounds differently than less frequent reset rules. In strong trends compounding helps; in choppy or mean-reverting markets it hurts. This is not a fee.

Inverse ETFs and log scale

Inverse leveraged ETFs can decay toward zero over long uptrends. The paths chart opens on log scale for inverse funds so the move from 100 to tiny values remains visible.

A synthetic leverage benchmark can go negative if the underlying move is large enough before the next reset; those points are omitted from the path chart because they are not investable account values.
